02 Calculation

Distance Calculations

To determine cluster membership, the Euclidean Distance metric was applied between every data point and each centroid.

Formula: d = √((x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)²)

Cluster Assignment Logic

Each record is assigned to the cluster whose centroid produces the minimum distance.

Example: If a point has distances (C1: 472, C2: 2472, C3: 6972), it will be assigned to Cluster 1.

Detailed Iteration Process

The K-Means algorithm works iteratively, refining cluster assignments and centroid positions at each step until convergence is achieved.

Iteration 1

In the first iteration, all data points are assigned to the nearest initial centroid. Since these centroids were predefined, this stage produces the highest variability.

Many points are still far from their optimal cluster, which results in higher variance.

Iteration 2

After recalculating centroids using the mean of assigned points, clusters are updated.

This significantly improves grouping accuracy and reduces the overall variance, as points begin to concentrate around more representative centers.

Iteration 3

In the third iteration, centroid movement becomes minimal and cluster assignments stabilize.

This indicates that the algorithm has reached convergence and further iterations would not significantly change the results.
